    Security Risk Assessment Report

    Scenario: St. Jude Hospital Electronic Health Record System

    by Eziuche Emmanuel

    Executive Summary

    This Security Risk Assessment Report evaluates the potential vulnerabilities, system vectors, and operational threats confronting the electronic health record system at St. Jude Hospital. Since this clinical computing infrastructure processes sensitive patient medical histories, private diagnostic results, and confidential treatment telemetry, safeguarding these system assets is crucial to maintaining operational continuity and compliance (Chang, 2022). This report identifies key threat vectors, highlights organizational infrastructural weak spots, and provides actionable, risk-aware architectural recommendations to secure patient storage databases, mitigate dangerous social engineering vectors, and protect clinical records throughout the analytical lifecycle.

    System/Scenario Overview

    The assessed system is an integrated, cloud-based hospital information environment hosting a Random Forest machine learning pipeline (Kalita, Bhattacharyya and Roy, 2024). This platform ingests structured patient telemetry, pharmacy allocations, and employee metrics via automated data pipelines. Users, including clinical executives, practicing physicians, and human resource administrators, interact with the system through local terminals, web interfaces, and secure mobile portals to evaluate upskilling requirements and patient care. The infrastructure relies on a centralized relational SQL database designed for structured, auditable patient record storage alongside distributed cloud microservices for model execution, predictive evaluation, and live inference.

    Identified Threats & Vulnerabilities

    A prominent operational threat is spear-phishing campaigns targeting clinical and administrative hospital accounts. Phishing exploits human psychological vulnerabilities, tricking healthcare staff into disclosing access credentials or executing malicious attachment payloads (Nielson, 2023). A compromised staff account grants external adversaries unrestricted access to the underlying machine learning training pipelines, enabling unauthorized structural manipulations (Akinsola and Ebenezer, 2021). Consequently, securing sensitive system personnel remains a fundamental operational requirement for modern medical enterprise environments.

    Another threat is malware, particularly ransomware. If malicious software infects the cloud environments hosting our clinical predictive systems, it can encrypt databases, freeze execution pipelines, and completely halt hospital operations. Malware propagates through unpatched endpoint vulnerabilities, exploiting network ports to execute remote code, which damages institutional trust and leads to substantial financial recovery overhead.

    Lastly, unauthorized data breaches and exfiltration present severe legal risks. Because the platform houses sensitive personal health records, unauthorized exposures directly violate Article 5 data minimisation principles under the General Data Protection Regulation (Walton, 2024). Breaches occur through poorly configured database instances, unprotected API endpoints, or weak data transmission protocols, leaving patient files exposed to malicious actors.

    Recommendations

    To mitigate these risks and secure our enterprise computing infrastructure, three essential security measures must be enforced. First, strong Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) must be mandated across all entry points, mitigating credential harvesting risks by requiring independent validation factors before gaining access (Nielson, 2023). Second, cryptographic encryption must be implemented for all datasets both at-rest and in-transit. Applying Advanced Encryption Standard (AES-256) for stored data and Transport Layer Security (TLS 1.3) for active API traffic prevents unauthorized data interception (Nielson, 2023). Third, the principle of least privilege must be applied to Identity and Access Management (IAM), restricting database access through strict role-based controls to reduce the internal attack surface and limit lateral movement if a perimeter breach occurs (Nielson, 2023).

    Conclusion

    In conclusion, modern analytical pipelines require comprehensive risk mitigation to secure corporate networks and protect proprietary algorithms (Manure and Bengani, 2023). Unpatched endpoints, human vulnerabilities, and unencrypted databases expose organizations to major security breaches. By enforcing MFA, database encryption, and least-privilege IAM controls, the enterprise secures its computational infrastructure, maintains user privacy, and ensures compliance with modern international data protection laws.
